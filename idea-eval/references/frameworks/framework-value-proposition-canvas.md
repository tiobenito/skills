# Value Proposition Canvas

**What it is:** A two-sided fit check. On one side, the user's jobs, pains, and gains. On the other, what your offering does, the pains it relieves, and the gains it creates. A strong idea has tight fit between the two.

**Best for:** Testing whether the value proposition actually lands with the target user.

## Running it

1. **Customer side** — list the user's jobs (what they're trying to do), pains (frustrations, risks, costs of the current way), and gains (the outcomes they actually want).
2. **Offering side** — list what your product/service does, the specific pains it removes (pain relievers), and the gains it produces (gain creators).
3. Draw the lines: does each pain reliever map to a real pain? Does each gain creator map to a wanted gain? Rank pains and gains by how much the user cares.
4. Look for the mismatches — relievers nobody needs, big pains you don't address.

## What to pull out

- Where the offering genuinely fits the user's top pains/gains.
- Where it doesn't (features chasing pains nobody has).
- The single most important pain or gain the idea should lead with.

## Watch for

- Inventing pains to justify features.
- Treating every pain as equally important — weight them.
